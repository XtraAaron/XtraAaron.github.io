Reverse Engineered Mechanical Dog

SolidWorks Linkage SAM 3D Printing Mechanical Design

Description

For the first ENSC 386 (Introduction to Mechanical Design ENSC 386) project, my group of 3 was tasked with reverse engineering the mechanisms behind the movement of a 1950s toy dog. The 3 main components of note to its movement were the jaw, the body, and the arms. All 3 movements can be seen in the video below, as part of the toy itself.

I designed 3 design candidates for the mechanism, particular for the body and the jaw. Most of the design and testing work for this was done in Linkage, with some SAM used initally before I had access to Linkage. I utilized (INERST HERE). All designs were made with the final assembly in mind, where I assisted in 3D printing a functioning prototype of the mechanism for testing and demonstration.

Challenges

Accurate Motion

The largest challenge was achieving motion that was very close to the automaton. As seen in the above videos, there is a slight delay at the bottom of the bowing motion. This problem was solved by precise engineering, where I created an inital prototype using a cam to replicate the motion, which then me and my team appropriated into a more practical design for the final assembly.


What I learned

This project expanded my mechanical design experience, as it was the first project that I could apply knowledge of studied mechanical designs and components in a greater mechanism. In addition, I learned about the patent system, as I used it to help research possible mechanisms for inspiration.
